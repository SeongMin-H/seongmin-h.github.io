Nanofluids & Colloidal Transport

I am an M.S. student in Robotics and Technology Convergence at Chung-Ang University, working in the Nanomechanics and Advanced Materials Laboratory under Prof. Sunghan Kim. Here is my CV.

I study how colloidal structure and interfacial gradients govern charge and heat transport, as well as rheological behavior, in carbon-based nanoparticle media. I connect these experiments with physics-based and data-driven models.

I showed that convection of graphene oxide particles under a temperature gradient generates electrical output that can drive a self-powered pressure sensor (ICHMT 2026, patent pending), and that sonication-tuned graphite/MWCNT networks lower the flow resistance and temperature sensitivity of hybrid nanofluids. Check out my Research Projects.

Outside the lab, I grew from team member (2019) to president and chassis team leader (2023) and acting faculty advisor (2025) of Chung-Ang University’s Formula Student Korea team.

Research

From microscopic organization to macroscopic performance

My research investigates how colloidal structure and interfacial gradients govern charge and heat transport as well as rheological behavior in carbon-based nanoparticle media. By combining nanomaterial fabrication, experimental characterization, mechanics-informed analysis, and computational modeling, I seek predictive links between microscopic organization and macroscopic performance.

Interfacial transport & energy conversion

Engineering nanofluid systems for convection-driven transport, energy harvesting, and self-powered sensing.

Colloidal & rheological engineering

Controlling multiscale nanoparticle networks for reduced flow resistance, thermal stability, and conductive transport.

Rotating thermo-fluidic system design

Integrating theoretical design, experimental validation, and computational analysis in nanofluid-enabled rotating systems.
